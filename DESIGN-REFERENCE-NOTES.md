# Band website reference study

Reviewed before the November 2026 visual revision.

1. [Blink-182](https://www.blink182.com/) — conversion-first structure; announcements and merchandise are immediately actionable.
2. [Green Day](https://greenday.com/) — highly visual campaign presentation with very little explanatory copy.
3. [Fall Out Boy](https://www.falloutboy.com/) — small branded header, dominant tour artwork, and extremely scannable ticket rows.
4. [Paramore](https://paramore.net/) — symbol-first identity, large fields of negative space, and photo-diary content that feels authored.
5. [My Chemical Romance](https://www.mychemicalromance.com/) — restrained navigation, strong texture, and one central campaign object rather than a busy collage.
6. [Sum 41](https://www.sum41.com/) — one dramatic choice and almost no competing homepage actions.
7. [All Time Low](https://www.alltimelow.com/) — current release artwork leads, followed by anniversary promotion, community signup, and tour.
8. [Simple Plan](https://officialsimpleplan.com/) — conventional navigation with tour, music, social, and newsletter utilities kept obvious.
9. [The Offspring](https://www.offspring.com/) — a release splash page with one album, one statement, and one listening action.
10. [Neck Deep](https://www.neckdeepuk.com/) — campaign artwork takes over the page; streaming, merchandise, and signup remain secondary.
11. [State Champs](https://statechamps.band/) — hero campaign, two direct actions, then merchandise, tour, videos, and follow/signup.
12. [A Day to Remember](https://adtr.com/) — compact navigation, full-screen media, and three clear destinations: dates, merch, and listening.
13. [Bring Me the Horizon](https://www.bmthofficial.com/) — a complete visual world with asymmetric navigation and deliberate typography rather than generic cards.
14. [Pierce the Veil](https://piercetheveil.net/) — commerce and tour information are treated as primary fan tasks.
15. [Jimmy Eat World](https://www.jimmyeatworld.com/) — intentionally era-specific visual language; every element belongs to the same design world.
16. [Weezer](https://weezer.com/) — symbolic identity, a single announcement bar, and very limited competing information.
17. [Foo Fighters](https://foofighters.com/) — immersive visual-first presentation with navigation kept out of the way.
18. [Queens of the Stone Age](https://qotsa.com/) — sparse, cinematic, campaign-led presentation.
19. [Taking Back Sunday](https://www.takingbacksunday.com/) — one anniversary release dominates the entrance with a direct store action.
20. [The Killers](https://www.thekillersmusic.com/) — full-bleed campaign art, prominent primary navigation, tour rows, videos, and email capture.
21. [Linkin Park](https://www.linkinpark.com/) — a clean symbol-led load state followed by a single current soundtrack campaign and a compact information architecture.

## Applied lessons

- Do not place the phoenix inside a decorative box or fake background. Let the transparent mark sit directly in the composition with negative space.
- Use a readable text wordmark in the navigation; reserve expressive typography for larger moments.
- Give the first viewport one job: establish the band, show the members, and offer listening or show actions.
- Keep the palette narrow and remove effects that do not reinforce the current campaign.
- Treat tour dates as practical rows with clear ticket actions.
- Let release artwork and photography provide the energy instead of adding rotation, oversized marks, and competing decorative layers.
- Keep each homepage section distinct: current release, band identity, live photography, shows, then contact.
